## Equipment and the rack

- **Table.** Six pockets, commonly 9 ft in competition.
- **Balls.** Ten object balls numbered 1–10, plus a cue ball.
- **The rack.** A triangle of ten, racked as tightly as possible, with the **1-ball at the apex on the foot spot** and the **10-ball in the middle of the triangle**. Everything else is placed at random.
- **The head string.** A line across the table a quarter of its length from the head rail. The break is played from behind it.

## Object of the game

Pocket the **10-ball in a called pocket** on a legal shot. That wins the rack, whenever it happens.

## The break

- The breaker plays from behind the head string, and must strike the **1-ball first**.
- If no ball is pocketed, **at least four object balls must reach a rail**, or the break is a foul.
- Balls pocketed on a legal break stay down and the breaker continues.
- **Pocketing the 10 on the break does not win.** It is spotted and the breaker plays on.
- After a foul on the break, the incoming player takes ball in hand.

That single difference reshapes the game. In nine-ball a lucky break can end the rack outright; here it cannot.

## The push out

On the shot immediately after a legal, foul-free break, the player at the table may announce a **push out**.

- The cue ball may be sent anywhere. It need not contact any ball, and nothing need reach a rail.
- Other fouls still apply, and the 10 pocketed on a push out is spotted.
- If no foul is committed, the **other player chooses who shoots next** — take the table, or hand it straight back.

## Calling shots

Every shot but the break must be called: the **ball and the pocket**.

- Obvious shots need not be called aloud, but the opponent may always ask what is intended.
- Details such as cushions, kisses and caroms need not be described.

**If you pocket a ball you did not call, your inning simply ends.** It is not a foul, and — unlike most call-shot games — **the ball stays down** rather than being spotted. The incoming player may then take the table as it lies, or **hand the shot back to you**.

> That option is the sting. Pot a ball by accident and leave a good position, and your opponent will simply give the table back and make you play from it.

## Rules of play

- On every shot, the **first ball the cue ball contacts** must be the **lowest-numbered ball on the table**. Cushions before that contact are fine — a kick off one or more rails is legal.
- Legally pocket the called ball and you continue at the table.
- Balls pocketed alongside a legally called ball stay down and count as progress.
- If nothing drops on a legal shot, the turn passes and the balls stay where they lie.

## Fouls

A foul ends the turn and gives the incoming player **ball in hand** — the cue ball may be placed anywhere on the table and played in any direction.

- Scratching, or driving the cue ball off the table.
- Failing to strike the lowest-numbered ball first.
- No ball pocketed and no ball driven to a rail after contact.
- Driving any ball off the table.
- Touching a ball with cue, hand or clothing; a double hit or a push shot.
- Shooting before all balls have come to rest, or out of turn.
- Having both feet off the floor at the moment of striking.

## Three consecutive fouls

Three fouls in a row by the same player, without a legal shot in between, is **loss of the rack**. The player must be warned that they are on two fouls before the third shot.

## Spotting the 10

The 10 is the only ball ever returned to the table. It is spotted when it has been pocketed:

- on the break shot,
- on a push out,
- on a foul,
- without being called,
- or in a pocket other than the one called,

and when it is driven off the table. It goes on the **foot spot**, or as near below it as possible on the long string towards the foot rail.

## Ten-ball and nine-ball compared

The two games share a skeleton. These are the differences that matter at the table.

| | Ten-ball | Nine-ball |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calling shots | Every shot | None — slop counts |
| Money ball on the break | Spotted, play continues | Wins the rack |
| Ball pocketed uncalled | Stays down, inning ends, opponent may pass it back | Counts, and you continue |
| Three consecutive fouls | Loss of rack | Loss of game |
| Push out | Yes | Yes |
| Foul penalty | Ball in hand anywhere | Ball in hand anywhere |
