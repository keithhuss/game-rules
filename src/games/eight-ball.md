## Equipment and the rack

- **Table.** Six pockets, commonly 7, 8 or 9 ft. Bar tables are usually 7 ft.
- **Balls.** Fifteen object balls plus a cue ball, 2¼ in (57.15 mm). Balls 1–7 are *solids*, 9–15 are *stripes*, and the 8 is black.
- **The rack.** A triangle, with the apex ball on the foot spot. The 8-ball goes in the centre of the third row. The two balls in the bottom corners must be one solid and one stripe. All other balls are placed at random.
- **The head string.** A line across the table a quarter of its length from the head rail. The area behind it is the *kitchen*, and the break is played from there.

## Object of the game

One side takes the solids, the other the stripes. A player must legally pocket all seven balls of their group and then legally pocket the 8-ball to win.

Groups are not decided by the rack or the break — see rule 4.

## The break

- The breaker places the cue ball anywhere behind the head string.
- **A legal break** must either pocket a ball, or drive at least four object balls to a rail. Anything less is a foul: the incoming player may accept the table as it lies, or re-rack and break themselves.
- **Balls pocketed on the break stay down**, and the breaker continues — but the table is still open.
- **Scratching on the break** is a foul. The incoming player takes the cue ball in hand behind the head string, and any balls pocketed stay down.
- **Pocketing the 8 on the break** is neither a win nor a loss. The breaker may either re-rack and break again, or have the 8 spotted and carry on.
- Driving an object ball off the table on the break is a foul, and the ball is not returned to the table.

## The open table and choosing groups

The table is **open** immediately after the break, even if balls were pocketed. While it is open:

- A player may shoot at any object ball except the 8.
- Striking the 8 first is a foul.

Groups are decided only when a player legally pockets a called ball after the break. That ball's group becomes theirs for the rest of the game; the other group goes to the opponent. If the called shot misses, the table stays open for the next player.

## Calling shots

Every shot but the break must be called — both the **ball** and the **pocket**.

- Details such as cushions, kisses and caroms need not be described.
- Obvious shots need not be called aloud, but the opponent may always ask what is intended.
- A ball pocketed other than as called does not count as a made shot, and the turn ends. The ball stays down.

## Legal shots and safeties

On every shot the cue ball must first strike a ball of the shooter's group — or, on an open table, any ball but the 8. Then either a ball must be pocketed, or **any** ball must reach a rail. Failing that, it is a foul.

A player who legally pockets a called ball continues at the table.

**Safeties.** A player may play for position rather than to score by announcing "safety" before the shot. The shot must still be legal. The turn then ends even if a ball was pocketed, and any of the shooter's balls that dropped stay down.

## Fouls

A foul ends the turn and gives the incoming player **ball in hand** — the cue ball may be placed anywhere on the table and played in any direction. The exception is a foul on the break, which gives ball in hand behind the head string.

- Scratching, or driving the cue ball off the table.
- Striking a ball outside your group first, or the 8 while the table is open.
- No ball pocketed and no ball driven to a rail.
- Driving any ball off the table.
- Touching a ball with cue, hand or clothing; a double hit or a push shot.
- Shooting before all balls have come to rest, or out of turn.
- Having both feet off the floor at the moment of striking.

Fouls do not carry a points penalty; the cost is ball in hand.

## Losing the game

A player loses immediately by:

- Pocketing the 8-ball before clearing their own group.
- Pocketing the 8-ball in a pocket other than the one called.
- Driving the 8-ball off the table.
- Fouling on the same shot that pockets the 8-ball — including scratching.

> Note the asymmetry: fouling while pocketing the 8 loses the game, but fouling at any other time merely hands over ball in hand. The last shot is the one to play carefully.

## Stalemate

If both players are unwilling to attempt a shot that might lose the game, and the referee judges that three consecutive turns each have produced no progress, the game is a stalemate. The balls are re-racked and the original breaker breaks again.

## Common house variations

None of these are WPA rules, but all are widespread. Agree which apply before you start.

| Variation | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Last pocket | The 8 must be pocketed in the same pocket as your final group ball. |
| Slop counts | Shots need not be called; anything pocketed stays down and the turn continues. |
| Scratch on the 8 loses | A scratch while pocketing the 8 loses even if the 8 was legally made. |
| Kitchen only | Ball in hand is restricted to behind the head string rather than anywhere. |
| 8 on the break wins | Pocketing the 8 on the break wins outright, rather than being re-racked. |
| Must hit a rail | A rail contact is required after every shot, not only when nothing is pocketed. |
