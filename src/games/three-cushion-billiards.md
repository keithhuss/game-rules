## Equipment and the table

- **Table.** **No pockets.** The match size is 2.84 × 1.42 m — ten foot by five — with a heated slate on competition tables to keep the cloth fast and consistent.
- **Balls.** Three, and larger than any pocket-game ball at **61.5 mm**: a red, and two cue balls, one white and one yellow. Each player owns one cue ball for the whole game.
- **The spots.** Three lie along the long axis: the head spot, the centre spot and the foot spot.
- **The rail sights.** Twenty diamonds — **seven along each long rail and three along each short rail**. A pocket table has only six per long rail, because the middle position is taken up by the side pocket. Here nothing interrupts them, and they are the basis of the *diamond system* players use to plan cushion routes.

## Object of the game

Score **counts**. One count is a single carom in which your cue ball touches **both** other balls, having contacted **three or more cushions** before it reaches the second of them.

There is no potting and there are no pockets. Every point in the game comes from that one stroke.

A game is played to an agreed number of counts — 15, 25 or 50 are common; professional matches are longer.

## Starting the game

- Players **lag**: each strokes a ball from the head string to the foot cushion and back. Whoever finishes nearer the head cushion wins, and may either break or hand the break to their opponent.
- **Opening position.** The red goes on the foot spot. The non-breaker's cue ball goes on the head spot. The breaker's cue ball is placed 18.25 cm to the right or left of it.
- **The break shot must strike the red ball first, and directly.** Failing to do so is a foul and the inning ends.
- After the break, either object ball may be struck first.

## Scoring a count

> A three-cushion billiard is valid and is a count of one when the cue ball has touched both object balls and has made three or more cushion impacts before striking the last object ball.

Reading that carefully is worth it, because the ordering is what people get wrong:

- The three cushions must come **before the second object ball is struck**, not before the first.
- The first object ball may be struck at any point — before any cushion, or after all three.
- **More than three cushions is fine.** Three is a minimum, not a target.
- Cushions contacted after the second object ball don't count towards anything.

A player who scores continues, and keeps shooting for as long as each stroke counts. The inning ends on the first stroke that fails to score.

## Fouls

A foul ends the inning. **No point is deducted, and the opponent gains nothing** — you simply stop shooting.

- Failing to strike the red first on the break shot.
- Striking the cue ball more than once, or with anything but the tip of the cue.
- A push shot.
- Shooting while any ball is still moving, out of turn, or with the wrong cue ball.
- Touching any ball with cue, hand or clothing.
- Having both feet off the floor at the moment of striking.
- Driving any ball off the table.

## Balls off the table, and frozen balls

- **A ball leaving the table** is a foul and the inning ends. The balls are then replaced on their spots.
- **A cue ball frozen to a cushion** may not be played into that cushion. Doing so is a foul. Play it away from the cushion instead, or have it respotted where the rules allow.

## Ending the game

The game ends when a player reaches the agreed number of counts. Under USBA rules that stands **even if the opponent has had one turn fewer** at the table; some formats instead grant an equalising inning, so agree which applies before you start.

## The other carom games

Three-cushion is the dominant form, but the family is older and larger. All use the same table and the same three balls; only the condition for a valid carom changes.

| Game | The count |
| --- | --- |
| Straight rail | Any carom counts — no cushion requirement at all. Skilled players could gather the balls in a corner and run hundreds, which is why the games below exist. |
| Balkline | Straight rail with the table divided into balk spaces by drawn lines. Only a limited number of consecutive shots may be played with both object balls inside one space, breaking up nursery gathering. |
| One-cushion | The cue ball must contact at least one cushion before the second object ball. The intermediate step between straight rail and three-cushion. |
| Artistic billiards | Set trick shots attempted from fixed positions, scored on difficulty. |
