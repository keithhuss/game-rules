## Equipment and the rack

- **Table.** Six pockets, commonly 8 or 9 ft. The full game is at its best on a 9 ft table.
- **Balls.** Fifteen object balls numbered 1–15, plus a cue ball. Numbers carry no value — every ball is worth exactly one point.
- **The opening rack.** All fifteen in a triangle, apex ball on the foot spot.
- **The head string.** A line across the table a quarter of its length from the head rail. The cue ball is played from behind it on the opening break and after a scratch.

## Object of the game

Score one point per ball, and reach an agreed total before your opponent. **100 or 125 points** are usual; championship matches are played to 150.

Because every ball counts the same, the game is not about which ball you pot — it is about never running out of position.

## The opening break

The opening break is a **safety**, not an attempt to score. The breaker must either:

- pocket a called ball, **or**
- drive the cue ball **and at least two object balls** to a rail.

Anything less is a **breaking foul**: two points are deducted, and the opponent may either accept the table as it lies or have the balls re-racked and make the offender break again.

## Scoring

- Every shot must be **called** — the ball and the pocket.
- A legally pocketed called ball scores **one point**, and the shooter continues.
- **Any additional balls pocketed on the same stroke also score one point each**, called or not.
- Balls pocketed on a foul do not score, and are spotted.

## The re-rack

This is the mechanism the game is named for, and the reason a turn need never end.

When **fourteen** balls have been pocketed, play pauses. Those fourteen are racked again in the triangle **with the apex position left empty**. The fifteenth ball — the **break ball** — stays exactly where it lies, as does the cue ball. The shooter then plays on.

If the **break ball or the cue ball lies inside the rack area**, it cannot simply stay there. The rules respot the offending ball — the break ball to the head spot, the cue ball to the centre spot — with a full table of cases in the WPA rules for when those spots are themselves occupied.

> Everything in straight pool is played backwards from this moment. A good player is not thinking about the ball they are potting but about which ball they will leave as the break ball, and from where. Leave yourself no break ball and a hundred-point run ends at fourteen.

## Legal shots and safeties

On every shot the cue ball must **contact an object ball**. Every ball is on in straight pool, so any of them will do — and the cue ball may reach it off one or more cushions on the way. A kick shot into your object ball is entirely legal.

After that contact, either a ball must be pocketed, or **any** ball — the cue ball included — must reach a rail. Failing that, it is a foul.

A player may play a **safety** deliberately, announcing it beforehand. The shot must still be legal, and the turn then ends. Any ball pocketed on a called safety is spotted and does not score.

## Fouls and penalties

A standard foul costs **one point** and ends the turn.

- Failing to contact any object ball.
- No ball pocketed and no ball driven to a rail.
- Scratching, or driving the cue ball off the table — after which the cue ball is **in hand behind the head string**.
- Driving any ball off the table.
- Touching a ball with cue, hand or clothing; a double hit or a push shot.
- Shooting before all balls have come to rest, or out of turn.
- Having both feet off the floor at the moment of striking.

Balls pocketed on a foul are spotted and score nothing.

## Three consecutive fouls

If a player commits **three standard fouls in a row**, without a scoring stroke in between:

- The third foul costs its usual one point, and then a further **fifteen points** are deducted.
- The foul count resets to zero.
- **All fifteen balls are re-racked**, and the offender must shoot again under the opening-break requirements.

The player must be **warned that they are on two fouls** before the third shot. A breaking foul does not count towards the three.

## Ending the game

The first player to reach the agreed total wins, and a game can finish mid-run — the moment the winning point is scored, the game is over.

Scores can go **negative**, since fouls deduct and the penalties are steep. A player on minus fifteen after an early three-foul is behind but not beaten; there are always fifteen more balls on the table.
