  - term: Frame

## Equipment and the table

- **Table.** 12ft × 6ft with six pockets, the same table as English billiards.
- **Balls.** Twenty-two, each 52.5 mm: a white cue ball, fifteen reds, and six colours — yellow, green, brown, blue, pink and black.
- **The baulk line and D.** The baulk line is drawn 29 in from the bottom cushion; the D is a semicircle of 11.5 in radius drawn into baulk from its centre. The cue ball is played from the D whenever it is in hand.
- **The spots.** Each colour has its own spot, shown below. Every colour starts on its own spot — including the pink, which sits on the pyramid spot.
- **The reds** are racked in a triangle **behind the pink**, in the space between the pink and the top cushion, with the apex red pointing back down the table and as close as possible to the pink without touching it. No red sits on a spot.

## Object of the game

Score more points than your opponent in a frame. Points come from potting balls, and from your opponent's fouls.

A match is the best of an agreed odd number of frames.

## Ball values

| Ball | Value |
| --- | ---: |
| <span class="dot red"></span>Red | 1 |
| <span class="dot yel"></span>Yellow | 2 |
| <span class="dot grn"></span>Green | 3 |
| <span class="dot brn"></span>Brown | 4 |
| <span class="dot blu"></span>Blue | 5 |
| <span class="dot pnk"></span>Pink | 6 |
| <span class="dot blk"></span>Black | 7 |

Fifteen reds, each answered by a black, plus the six colours cleared at the end, gives the maximum break of **147**.

## The sequence of play

The striker plays the first stroke of the frame with the cue ball in hand, and a red is the ball on.

- **Pot a red.** It scores 1 and stays off the table.
- **Then a colour.** Name the colour you intend — from that moment it alone is the ball on, and the other colours are not. Pot it for its value and it is **returned to its spot**.
- **Then another red**, and so on. Reds and colours alternate for as long as reds remain.

A visit ends when the striker fails to pot the ball on, or fouls. If a red is not potted, the next player's ball on is a red again.

> The alternation is what makes position play matter. Potting a red is easy; potting a red *and* leaving the cue ball on a colour, and that colour so as to leave the next red, is the whole game.

## The final sequence

After the last red has been potted and the colour following it has been taken, the six colours must be potted in **ascending order** — yellow, green, brown, blue, pink, black.

From this point the colours are **not** respotted; each stays down once legally potted. Potting the black ends the frame.

## Fouls

A foul ends the visit. The penalty goes to the opponent as points — see rule 7.

- **Failing to strike a ball that is on first.** Missing every ball counts, and so does striking any other ball first. It is the first *ball* contacted that matters — cushions struck on the way are irrelevant, so a kick out of a snooker is legal. While reds are on, *any* red is a legal first contact; when a colour is on, only the nominated colour is — hitting a different colour first is a foul even if you pot it.
- Potting a ball that is not on.
- Potting the cue ball, or driving it off the table.
- Forcing any ball off the table.
- Striking the cue ball more than once, playing a push stroke, or playing a jump shot.
- Playing before all balls have come to rest, out of turn, or with the wrong ball.
- Touching any ball with cue, hand or clothing.
- Having both feet off the floor at the moment of striking.

## Penalties and the incoming player's options

The penalty is **four points**, or the value of the ball on or the ball improperly struck or potted, **whichever is highest**. So the minimum penalty is 4 and the maximum is 7.

After a foul, the incoming player may either play from where the balls lie, or **require the offender to play again** from that position. Reds potted on a foul stay down; colours potted on a foul are respotted.

## Free ball

If a foul leaves the striker **snookered** — unable to strike either extreme edge of any ball on in a straight line — the referee declares a **free ball**.

- The striker may nominate any ball as the ball on for that stroke.
- The nominated ball counts as the value of the ball on, not its own value.
- If the nominated ball is potted, it is respotted and its value as the ball on is scored.
- It is a foul to use a free ball to snooker the opponent when only the pink and black remain.

## Foul and a miss

If the striker fails to hit the ball on and the referee judges that a genuine attempt was not made, a **miss** is called in addition to the foul.

The incoming player may then also require the shot to be **replayed from the original position**, giving the offender another attempt at the same problem.

Where the striker was not snookered and a full ball was available, a **third consecutive miss loses the frame**. The player must be warned after the second.

## Respotting colours

A colour that must return to the table goes on its own spot. If that spot is occupied:

- It goes on the **highest-value spot** that is free.
- If every spot is occupied, it is placed as close as possible to its own spot, on the line between that spot and the top cushion, without touching another ball.
- If there is no room on that side, it goes as close as possible on the other side.

## Ending the frame

The frame ends when the black is potted, or when a foul is committed with only the black remaining.

If the scores are level after the black has been potted, the **black is respotted**, players draw lots for who plays, and the striker plays from in hand. The next score or foul decides the frame.

A player may concede at any time when it is their turn.

## Shortened and casual formats

Widely played, none of them WPBSA frame rules. Agree the format before you start.

| Format | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Six-red snooker | Six reds instead of fifteen. Much shorter frames; everything else is unchanged. |
| Ten-red / snooker plus | Intermediate red counts, common on smaller tables. |
| No miss rule | Fouls are still fouls, but the shot is never replayed. Usual in casual play. |
| Concede at a frame ball | The trailing player concedes once the deficit exceeds the points left on the table. |
| Free ball ignored | Casual games often skip the free ball and simply hand over the penalty. |
