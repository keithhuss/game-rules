## Equipment and markings

- **Table.** Full size is 12ft × 6ft with six pockets — the same table as snooker. The game is also played on smaller tables.
- **Balls.** Three, each 52.5 mm: one red and two cue balls, white and yellow. In older sets the second cue ball is a white with a spot.
- **Baulk line.** Drawn across the table 29 in from the bottom cushion. Everything between that line and the bottom cushion is *baulk*.
- **The D.** A semicircle of 11.5 in radius, centred on the baulk line and drawn into baulk. The cue ball is placed here when played from hand.
- **Spots.** Three are used: the *billiard spot*, 12.75 in from the top cushion (snooker's black spot); the *pyramid spot* (snooker's pink); and the *centre spot*.

## Object of the game

Score points with cannons, pots and in-offs. A player continues at the table for as long as each stroke scores; the visit ends the moment a stroke scores nothing or fouls.

A match is played to an agreed total — 100, 200 or 300 up are common — or to the highest score within a fixed time.

## Starting play

- Toss or string for the choice of breaking. Each player keeps the same cue ball for the whole game.
- The red is placed on the billiard spot. Both cue balls start off the table.
- The breaker plays *from hand*: cue ball placed anywhere within or on the line of the D. Only the red is on the table, so the opening stroke must strike it.
- The second player likewise enters the game from hand when their turn comes.

## Scoring strokes

| Stroke | Points |
| --- | ---: |
| <span class="dot both"></span>Cannon — cue ball strikes both other balls | 2 |
| <span class="dot red"></span>Pot the red | 3 |
| <span class="dot wht"></span>Pot the opponent's cue ball | 2 |
| <span class="dot red"></span>In-off the red | 3 |
| <span class="dot wht"></span>In-off the opponent's cue ball | 2 |

Scores made in a single stroke are added together. A cannon followed by potting the red is 5; a cannon plus an in-off the red is also 5. The maximum from one stroke is **10** — cannon, both object balls potted, and the cue ball in-off the red.

The value of an in-off is set by the *first* ball struck: red first scores 3, the other cue ball first scores 2. If both are struck simultaneously it counts as the red.

## Spotting and balls off the table

### The red
Always returns to the table. It goes on the billiard spot; if that is occupied, the pyramid spot; if that is occupied too, the centre spot.

### The spot-stroke limit
If the red is potted twice in succession directly from the billiard spot, it is placed on the centre spot for the third.

### A cue ball
Never spotted. A cue ball that is potted, or goes in-off, stays off the table until its owner's next turn, when it re-enters from hand.

> Potting your opponent's cue ball does more than score 2 — it denies them position, since they must start their next visit from the D.

## Playing from hand

- Place the cue ball anywhere within or on the line of the D and play up the table.
- The cue ball must leave baulk before it may strike a ball lying in baulk. It must first hit a ball outside baulk, or a cushion beyond the baulk line.
- If the striker is in hand and every object ball lies in baulk, they may ask for the red to be spotted.

## Fouls

A foul ends the visit and cancels anything scored on that stroke. Unlike snooker, no penalty points go to the opponent. The incoming player takes the balls as they lie, or plays from hand if their own ball is off the table.

- Failing to strike any object ball.
- Forcing the cue ball, or any ball, off the table.
- A push stroke, a double hit, or a jump shot.
- Playing before all balls have come to rest.
- Striking with anything other than the tip of the cue, or touching a ball with cue, hand or clothing.
- Playing out of turn, or with the wrong cue ball.
- Having both feet off the floor at the moment of striking.
- Breaching the baulk restriction in rule 6.

## Limits on repetition

Three restrictions exist to stop a skilled player from running an unbreakable sequence of near-identical strokes.

### Hazards — 15
No more than fifteen consecutive pots and in-offs. The next scoring stroke must include a cannon.

### Cannons — 75
No more than seventy-five consecutive cannons. The next scoring stroke must include a hazard.

### Cannons in baulk — 2
After two consecutive cannons made with both object balls in baulk, the following stroke must send at least one ball out of baulk.

## Touching ball

If the cue ball comes to rest touching an object ball, the striker must play away from that ball without moving it. The touching ball does not count as having been struck, so a cannon requires contact with the other ball.
