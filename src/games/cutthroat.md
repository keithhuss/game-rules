## Equipment and the rack

- **Table.** Six pockets, any size.
- **Balls.** All fifteen object balls plus a cue ball.
- **Players.** Three. Five-player and other divisions exist — see rule 9.
- **The groups.** Low **1–5**, mid **6–10**, high **11–15**.
- **The rack.** A normal triangle with the **1 on the foot spot** at the apex, and the **6 and 11 in the two back corners** — one ball from each group at each point of the triangle, so the break scatters all three sets.

## Object of the game

Be the **last player with at least one ball still on the table**. You do that by pocketing everyone else's balls while protecting your own.

Note what this is not: you never try to clear your own group. Your five balls are your lives.

## Claiming the groups

The sets start **open** — nobody owns anything until balls start dropping.

- The groups settle once balls from **two** of the three sets have been pocketed. Whoever has been eating into the other two sets is, by elimination, left with the third.
- Many tables instead play that the **first player to legally pocket a ball claims whichever set they like** — usually the one they've just damaged least.

Agree which of those you're using before the break. It changes the whole opening.

## Play

- The breaker plays from behind the head string.
- **Your turn continues for as long as each shot legally pockets at least one ball.** Miss, and play passes to the left.
- Whether shots must be **called** is agreed beforehand — cutthroat is commonly played as slop.
- Pocketing more than one opponent's ball on a stroke is fine, and all of them stay down.

## Cutting your own throat

You may pocket a ball from **your own group**, and it counts as a legal shot — your turn continues.

> This is the move the game is named for. It keeps you at the table, and it costs you one of your five lives. Sometimes it is the only legal shot available; sometimes it is worth it to stay in and finish someone else off.

## Fouls and scratches

- **A scratch** — pocketing the cue ball or driving it off the table — is the serious one. **Every opponent spots one of their pocketed balls back onto the table.** Your turn ends and the next player plays from behind the head string.
- **Any other foul** simply ends your turn. The next player takes the cue ball where it lies, with no ball in hand.

The scratch penalty is what keeps cutthroat from being decided early. A player who is one ball from elimination is rescued by any careless scratch, from anybody.

## Elimination and coming back

When you have **no balls left on the table you are out**, and you stop taking turns.

But you are not necessarily finished. If an opponent scratches while you are out, one of your balls comes back and **you rejoin the game**. That remains possible right up until the moment someone wins.

## Winning

The **last player with a ball on the table** wins. If a single stroke clears the last balls of two players at once, the survivor still wins outright.

## Where rules vary

Cutthroat is a casual game with no governing body, and almost every table plays it slightly differently. Settle these first.

| Point | The variation |
| --- | --- |
| Claiming groups | Sets settle once two have been broken into, or the first player to pocket claims a set outright. |
| Call shot | Usually slop. Some play full call shot, ball and pocket. |
| Scratch penalty | One ball back per opponent is standard. Some spot only one ball total, or none if the scratcher has no balls left. |
| Ball in hand | Behind the head string after a scratch is usual; some play ball in hand anywhere. |
| Five players | Groups of three: 1–3, 4–6, 7–9, 10–12, 13–15. Four or six players divide similarly. |
| Break requirements | Rarely specified. Most tables ask only that the rack be struck and some ball reach a rail. |
