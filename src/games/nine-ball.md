## Equipment and the rack

- **Table.** Six pockets, commonly 7, 8 or 9 ft.
- **Balls.** Nine object balls numbered 1–9, plus a cue ball.
- **The rack.** A diamond, with the **1-ball at the apex on the foot spot** and the **9-ball in the centre**. The remaining balls are placed at random. All balls should be frozen together.
- **The head string.** A line across the table a quarter of its length from the head rail. The break is played from behind it.

## Object of the game

Pocket the **9-ball** on a legal shot. That is the entire object — the player who pockets the 9 wins, regardless of how many balls each side has pocketed up to that point.

Because the 9 can be sunk by a combination off the lowest ball, a game can end on the first shot after the break, or at any point thereafter.

## The break

- The breaker places the cue ball anywhere behind the head string.
- **A legal break** must strike the 1-ball first, and then either pocket a ball or drive at least four object balls to a rail.
- **Balls pocketed on a legal break stay down**, and the breaker continues.
- **Pocketing the 9 on the break wins the game** outright.
- **A foul on the break** — scratching, missing the 1, or failing to move four balls to a rail — gives the incoming player ball in hand anywhere. Balls pocketed stay down, and the 9 is spotted if it was among them.
- An object ball driven off the table is a foul and is not returned to the table, unless it is the 9.

## The push out

On the shot immediately after a legal break — and only then — the player at the table may announce a **push out**.

- The cue ball may be driven anywhere. It need not contact the lowest ball, and nothing need reach a rail.
- All other fouls still apply: scratching on a push out is a foul.
- Balls pocketed on a push out do not count and stay down, except the 9, which is spotted.
- After the push out, the incoming player chooses either to shoot, or to pass the shot back to the player who pushed.

A push out is the standard answer to a break that leaves no shot on the lowest ball.

## Rules of play

- On every shot, the **first ball the cue ball contacts** must be the **lowest-numbered ball on the table**. Cushions before that contact are fine — a kick off one or more rails into the lowest ball is legal.
- After that first contact, **any** ball that drops counts and stays down — including the 9.
- **Nine-ball is not a call-shot game.** Balls pocketed by accident, by kiss or by combination all count exactly as intended ones do.
- A player who legally pockets any ball continues at the table.
- If nothing is pocketed on a legal shot, the turn passes and the balls stay where they lie.

> This is why nine-ball rewards safety play so heavily. Since anything that drops counts, leaving your opponent hooked behind a ball is often worth more than a low-percentage pot.

## Fouls

A foul ends the turn and gives the incoming player **ball in hand** — the cue ball may be placed anywhere on the table and played in any direction. This applies to fouls on the break too.

- Scratching, or driving the cue ball off the table.
- Failing to strike the lowest-numbered ball first.
- No ball pocketed and no ball driven to a rail after contact.
- Driving any ball off the table.
- Touching a ball with cue, hand or clothing; a double hit or a push shot.
- Shooting before all balls have come to rest, or out of turn.
- Having both feet off the floor at the moment of striking.

Balls pocketed on a foul stay down. Only the 9 is ever returned to the table.

## The three-foul rule

If a player commits **three fouls in a row**, without a legal shot in between, they lose the game.

The fouls must be consecutive for that player, and the player must be **warned that they are on two fouls** before playing the third shot. Without that warning the count resets rather than the game ending.

## Spotting the 9

The 9-ball is the only ball that ever returns to the table. It is placed on the foot spot when it has been pocketed on a foul, on a push out, or driven off the table.

If the foot spot is occupied, the 9 goes as close to it as possible on the long string, toward the foot rail.

## Common house variations

None of these are WPA rules, but all are played somewhere. Agree which apply before you rack.

| Variation | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| No push out | The shot after the break follows the normal rules. |
| 9 on the break does not win | The 9 is spotted and play continues. |
| Call the 9 | The winning shot on the 9 must be called; the rest of the game stays slop. |
| Call shot throughout | Every pot must be called, as in eight-ball. |
| No three-foul rule | Consecutive fouls carry no extra penalty. |
| Kitchen only | Ball in hand is restricted to behind the head string. |
