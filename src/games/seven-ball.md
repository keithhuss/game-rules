## Equipment and the rack

- **Table.** Six pockets, any size.
- **Balls.** Seven object balls numbered 1–7, plus a cue ball.
- **The rack.** A **hexagon**: the 1-ball at the apex on the foot spot, the **7-ball in the centre**, and the remaining five placed clockwise around it.
- **The head string.** A line across the table a quarter of its length from the head rail. The break is played from behind it.

## Object of the game

Pocket the balls in rotation — the cue ball must always strike the **lowest-numbered ball** on the table first — and win by legally pocketing the **7-ball in one of your own three pockets**.

Balls 1 to 6 may be pocketed in **any** pocket. Only the 7 is restricted.

## The break

- The breaker places the cue ball anywhere behind the head string.
- **A legal break** must strike the 1-ball first, and then either pocket a ball or drive at least four object balls to a rail.
- Balls pocketed on a legal break stay down and the breaker continues.
- **Pocketing the 7 on a legal break wins the game.**
- If the 7 drops on a break where a foul also occurred, the 7 is spotted and the incoming player takes ball in hand.

## Choosing sides

Immediately after the break, the **non-breaker elects the three pockets along one long rail**. The breaker is automatically assigned the three along the opposite rail.

This is the whole difference from nine-ball. Position on the 7 is not enough — you need position on the 7 *relative to your own half of the table*.

## The push out

On the shot immediately after the break — and only then, once per game — the player at the table may announce a **push out**.

- The cue ball may be sent anywhere. It need not contact any ball, and nothing need reach a rail.
- A scratch is still a foul.
- It must be announced beforehand, or the stroke counts as an ordinary shot.

## Rules of play

- On every shot, the cue ball must strike the **lowest-numbered ball** on the table first.
- After that contact, any ball that drops counts and stays down.
- **Not a call-shot game.** Combinations, kisses and accidental pots all count.
- A player who legally pockets any ball continues at the table.
- If nothing drops on a legal shot, the turn passes and the balls stay as they lie.

## Fouls

A foul ends the turn and gives the incoming player **ball in hand** — the cue ball may be placed anywhere and played in any direction.

- Scratching, or driving the cue ball off the table.
- Failing to strike the lowest-numbered ball first.
- No ball pocketed and no ball driven to a rail after contact.
- Driving any ball off the table.
- Touching a ball with cue, hand or clothing; a double hit or a push shot.
- Shooting before all balls have come to rest, or out of turn.
- Having both feet off the floor at the moment of striking.

Balls pocketed on a foul **stay down** — they are not returned to the table.

## Winning and losing on the 7

The 7 is where seven-ball is least like nine-ball, and the penalties are severe.

| Outcome | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 7 pocketed in one of your own three pockets | **Win** |
| 7 pocketed on a legal break | **Win** |
| 7 pocketed in any pocket on the opponent's side | **Loss** |
| Scratching on the shot that pockets the 7 | **Loss** |
| 7 pocketed on a break where a foul also occurred | 7 spotted, opponent takes ball in hand |

> Note how unforgiving this is compared with nine-ball, where an early or fouled money ball is simply respotted. Here, sending the 7 down the wrong side of the table ends the game against you.

## Where sources differ

Seven-ball is a modern invented game with no governing body, so published rules are not fully consistent. Agree these before you play.

| Point | The variation |
| --- | --- |
| Passing back a push out | Nine-ball lets the incoming player hand the shot back to whoever pushed. Sources on seven-ball generally do not mention this either way. |
| Three-foul rule | Nine-ball loses the game on three consecutive fouls. Seven-ball rule sets generally omit it. |
| An illegal 7 without a scratch | Commonly stated as an outright loss, as above. Some casual play instead spots the 7 and continues. |
| Side selection | Usually the non-breaker elects. Some houses let the breaker choose, or fix the sides before the break. |
